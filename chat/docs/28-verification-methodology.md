# Verification methodology and what was rerun

## Historical research discipline

The supplied bundles separate candidate discovery from checking. Positive graphs are explicit edge lists or models that can be expanded edge-for-edge. Forbidden-cycle witnesses list distinct vertices and are checked against actual adjacency. Small templates are independently enumerated for internal cycles and terminal paths; composite proofs rely on an exact routing decomposition.

Several arguments are infinite statements with finite ingredients: the four-turn lemma, variable-factor transmission, phase-hole arithmetic, heptagon classification, turn-charge theorem, bridge extension and triangle grammar induction. The verifiers check their finite data and regressions; the written arguments carry the unbounded conclusions. No proof-assistant claim is made.

Exhaustive catalogue or search claims need both a domain argument and a completed generator or closure. Rejection ledgers prove that their listed candidates fail; a list alone does not establish completeness. For the 181-cell census, suppression to small cubic multigraphs supplies the domain argument. The 554-cell catalogue explicitly makes no order-exhaustive claim. The strip rejection array plus all reachable states supplies a finite closure check.

## Independent algorithms

The gadget stage compares compressed coefficient counts with an unrelated full-graph canonical DFS counter. The gap64 stage checks voltage/cell constraints and separately searches the weighted 1,890-vertex core without referring to the voltage list. Interface oracles are compared with expanded-graph spectra, including simultaneous multi-path states. The joint learner has a verifier that reconstructs routing truth tables without Z3. Identification witnesses are reconstructed from the explicitly stored seed and matching, including collision guards.

The joint source record also reports 1,252 nonempty graph-atlas controls and 7,512 full count comparisons for its optional C++ oracle, plus capped-count/timeout and Z3 export/reload regressions. These historical control logs are retained. Packaging reran the mathematical certificate programs listed below, not every optional control or optimizer.

Twenty-one central graph files were parsed independently for header consistency, duplicate edges, loops, degree distribution and connectivity. For graphs below 1,000 vertices, vertex/edge connectivity was computed. The independent canonical C++ counter was compiled from the retained source and run for selected exact lengths. `graph_checks.json` records each actual count, output string, or timeout; no filename is accepted as proof of its advertised score.

The 26,550-vertex graph exceeds that counter's built-in n<=10,000 input limit. Its forbidden-length gap is established by the exact structural verifier instead; do not edit a record to imply a direct brute-force count was executed. Larger finite absence proofs can legitimately use complete structural decompositions.

## Operational safety

Do not run assertion-based verifiers with Python optimization enabled. Original discovery sources may have old scratch paths or local dependency assumptions; portable wrapper programs do not silently patch them. Fresh verifier output belongs under an ignored output directory, not over historical logs. Outer and inner manifests deliberately separate new packaging files from preserved evidence.

A timeout is UNKNOWN. A count capped at K means at least K if the cap is reached. A printed hash establishes only content identity. Numerical solver feasibility is checked on the returned discrete assignment; solver optimality is not used to imply global graph minimality.
