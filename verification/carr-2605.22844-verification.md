# Verification: Carr, "Every Minimal Counterexample to the Erdős–Gyárfás Conjecture is Predominantly Cubic" (arXiv:2605.22844v1, May 2026)

**Verdict: every stated result is CORRECT.** The proofs are elementary and I re-derived
each independently. One bound in the paper is not tight: its own two corollaries
combine to give a strictly stronger constant (2/3 instead of 4/7) in one line — see §3.

Setting: a *minimal counterexample* is a graph G with δ(G) ≥ 3 and no cycle of length
a power of two, of minimum order, and of minimum size among those of minimum order.

## 1. Statement-by-statement check

**Lemma 0.1** (every proper subgraph H ⊊ G has δ(H) ≤ 2). ✔ Correct.
H precedes G in the (order, size) lexicographic order; if δ(H) ≥ 3, minimality of G
forces H to contain a power-of-2 cycle, which would also lie in G. The lexicographic
minimality is exactly what makes both deletion arguments below work (vertex deletion
reduces order; edge deletion keeps order, reduces size).

**Corollary 0.1(1)** (every vertex is adjacent to a vertex of degree exactly 3). ✔
G−v is proper, so some u has deg_{G−v}(u) ≤ 2; since deg_G(u) ≥ 3, u must have lost
an edge to v, and deg_G(u) ≤ 3, hence exactly 3. (Needs n ≥ 2, guaranteed by δ ≥ 3.)

**Corollary 0.1(2)** (vertices of degree ≥ 4 form an independent set). ✔
Deleting an edge joining two such vertices leaves a proper subgraph with δ ≥ 3,
contradicting Lemma 0.1. — This is the observation the paper correctly attributes to
Markström (*Congr. Numer.* 171 (2004), §4). Note: Markström's own statement of it
contains a typo ("If d(u) ≥ 3 and d(v) ≥ 3 then {u,v} can not be an edge" — the
hypotheses should read ≥ 4); his conclusion (independent set V₁ of degree-≥4 vertices,
nonempty V₂ of degree-3 vertices) is the correct one, and Carr's version is stated
correctly.

**Corollary 0.2** (a regular minimal counterexample is cubic). ✔ Immediate.

**Theorem 0.1** (at least 4/7 of the vertices have degree exactly 3). ✔ The counting
(4|V₄₊| ≤ e(V₃,V₄₊) ≤ 3|V₃|) is valid. But see below — 3|V₃| is not the right
upper bound to use, given the paper's own Corollary 0.1(1).

## 2. Context

The independence of V₄₊ (hence "regular ⟹ cubic") is from Markström 2004; Royle's
~2004 search already exploited it. Genuinely new in Carr: Corollary 0.1(1) (the
cubic-neighbor property) and the density bound of Theorem 0.1.

## 3. Strengthening: the paper's own results give 2/3, not just 4/7

By Corollary 0.1(1), **every cubic vertex has at least one cubic neighbor**, so each
v ∈ V₃ sends at most 2 of its 3 edges to V₄₊:

    4|V₄₊|  ≤  e(V₃, V₄₊)  ≤  2|V₃|
    ⟹  |V₄₊| ≤ |V₃|/2
    ⟹  |V₃| ≥ (2/3)·|V(G)|   >   (4/7)·|V(G)|.

Equality analysis: |V₃| = 2n/3 would force every V₄₊ vertex to have degree exactly 4,
every V₃ vertex to have exactly one V₃-neighbor (a perfect matching on V₃) and exactly
two V₄₊-neighbors.

## 4. Consequences used by this campaign's searches

For *minimum-order* counterexample searches at each order n, it is sound to restrict to
graphs satisfying: δ ≥ 3, no C₄ (4 = 2² is itself a forbidden cycle), V₄₊ independent,
every vertex adjacent to a degree-3 vertex, and |V₃| ≥ ⌈2n/3⌉. (If any counterexample
exists on ≤ N vertices, the global minimum-order one satisfies all of these and is
found at its order.) Our primary geng- and SMS-based sweeps deliberately do NOT assume
these (they decide the full min-degree-3 space — a stronger, assumption-free result);
the structural constraints are reserved for the auxiliary CEGAR cross-checks, where
they are stated as such.

One additional fact, proved and used for the geng track (it needs no minimality):
in any C₄-free graph with δ ≥ 3, Σ_{u∈N(v)}(deg(u)−1) ≤ n−1 for every v (the sets
N(u)∖{v}, u ∈ N(v), are pairwise disjoint, else a C₄ arises), hence Δ(G) ≤ ⌊(n−1)/2⌋.
